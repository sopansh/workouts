## Who this is for

Healthy adults (18 and over) who bowl pace or seam at recreational or club level and have been cleared to exercise. This page is education and a selection of existing exercises. It is not a programme shown to work for bowlers.

It is not for under-18s. Young bowlers are the group most studied for back injury and need qualified supervision (see the [youth consensus claim](/strength/cricket/#claim-youth-training-consensus) on the overview page). It is not for anyone with a current or recent injury, back pain that has not been assessed, a recent operation, pregnancy or the first months after birth, or a heart, lung, joint or nerve condition. Elite and professional bowlers should work with a qualified coach.

## What the evidence says

Almost all of it is from professional, academy or young bowlers. The population is stated on each claim in the evidence list at the foot of the page. Read the grade and the caveats before the sentence.

**Does strength work change bowling speed?**

- One small comparison of adult male club pace bowlers (18 men, 4 weeks) found a 6% higher ball release velocity after a combined programme of core and lower-body strength, plyometrics and weighted-implement training, and no change in the comparison group ([claim](#claim-cricket-club-pace-combined-training), grade C). Four kinds of training were mixed, the exercises are not in the abstract, and injuries were not measured. It is a reason to test the idea, not a result to expect.
- In 10 elite-academy pace bowlers, 8 weeks of off-season strength training raised strength measures but did not change release speed. The authors say strength built without skills practice neither helped nor harmed speed ([claim](#claim-cricket-offseason-strength-pace-bowlers), grade C, no control group).
- In 28 high-performance fast bowlers, shoulder extension strength was one of four variables that went with release speed, and no other knee or shoulder strength measure was named ([claim](#claim-cricket-bowling-speed-correlates), grade C, correlation only).
- On the plyometrics side, a 12-week plyometric-based programme in 42 professional fast bowlers improved jump and eccentric knee strength tests ([claim](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-plyometric-bowlers)), and an 8-week exercise programme in adolescent pace bowlers was associated with better results on several strength and endurance tests ([claim](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-ipp-risk-factors), grade B, adolescents, so not adult evidence). Neither measured injuries.

**The lower back**

- Lumbar stress fractures and back injuries are common in young and professional fast bowlers ([young bowlers](#claim-cricket-back-injuries); [professional bowlers](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-lumbar-stress-professional)).
- A 2023 systematic review of 25 studies found conflicting results and no study at low risk of bias, so what makes a bowler's back more or less tolerant of load is not settled ([claim](#claim-cricket-lbp-intrinsic-review), contested). An earlier review reported moderate evidence for bone stress on MRI as a risk factor and for increased shoulder counter-rotation as associated with back pain, and found the intervention studies too weak to conclude ([claim](#claim-cricket-lbp-risk-interventions), grade C). Counter-rotation is a bowling-technique matter for a coach.
- Trunk strength and endurance are therefore not shown to protect the back. In professional fast bowlers, trunk side strain was not linked with side-bridge endurance ([claim](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-side-strain)). A meta-analysis found core training changes trunk-strength tests more than it changes athletic performance ([claim](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-core-limits), grade A, trained people aged 16 to 44).

**Workload**

- Both a sudden rise in bowling and very little bowling were associated with injury in professional bowlers ([spikes](#claim-cricket-workload-spikes); [low and high](#claim-cricket-workload-low-and-high)). The pattern differs by tissue: tendon injuries followed sudden increases, and bone stress followed high medium-term load and low career load ([claim](#claim-cricket-workload-tissue-types), grade C).
- A systematic review of eight articles found the research weak, with an association between workload and lower-back injury of minimal strength ([claim](#claim-cricket-workload-lbp-review)). The ratios used to monitor load are contested ([claim](#claim-acwr-contested)).
- The overs and delivery counts in these studies describe the groups studied. They are not limits for you, and this page gives no number.

**The hamstrings**

- In Australian professionals, being a fast bowler carried a relative risk of 2.5 (95% CI 1.3 to 4.5) of a match time-loss hamstring injury, and a previous hamstring injury was also a risk factor ([claim](#claim-cricket-hamstring-risk-factors), grade C).
- Including the Nordic hamstring exercise in injury prevention programmes was associated with fewer hamstring injuries, in several sports and mostly football, not cricket ([claim](#claim-nordic-hamstring-injury), grade A). In cricketers, eccentric hamstring strength on that test did not differ by skill level or between bowlers and batters ([claim](#claim-cricket-eccentric-hamstring-levels), grade C), and UK professional clubs rarely ran the full programme ([survey](#claim-cricket-nordic-uptake), grade D).

## A short strength block (a convention)

This is a selection of exercises already on this site, listed with the reason and the state of the evidence for each. The selection is a coaching convention. It is not a study result, and no source shows that it improves bowling or lowers injury. Doses, loads and progression come from each exercise's page and from the [volume](/strength/principles/volume/) and [progression](/strength/principles/progression/) principles. This page sets none.

A convention for frequency: up to two short gym sessions a week, four to six exercises from this list in each, and a rest day before a heavy bowling day. Season placement is on the [season planning page](/strength/cricket/season-planning/).

- **[Goblet squat](/strength/exercises/goblet-squat/).** A foundation-level squat for the legs that brake and drive in the delivery stride. Evidence: a hypothesis. The adult club trial and the academy study used lower-body strength work, but their exercises are not known.
- **[Bulgarian split squat](/strength/exercises/bulgarian-split-squat/).** Single-leg strength for the front leg. Evidence: a hypothesis.
- **[Barbell hip thrust](/strength/exercises/hip-thrust/).** Hip extension strength, with a low spinal-load rating on its page. Evidence: a hypothesis.
- **[Seated leg curl](/strength/exercises/seated-leg-curl/).** Knee-flexor work on a machine, because hamstring injuries cluster in fast bowlers. Evidence: no trial in cricketers.
- **[Nordic hamstring curl](/strength/exercises/nordic-curl/) (advanced, optional).** The only exercise here with graded injury-outcome evidence, from other sports. Introduce it gradually, because it causes heavy soreness when started.
- **[Side plank](/strength/exercises/side-plank/) and [Pallof press](/strength/exercises/pallof-press/).** Trunk endurance and resisting rotation, since fast bowling combines side-flexion and rotation. Evidence: none in cricket that either prevents back or side-strain injury. If your back is not settled, use the [back-safe core guide](/core/) first.
- **[Chest-supported row](/strength/exercises/chest-supported-row/) and [Face pull](/strength/exercises/face-pull/).** Upper-back and rear-shoulder balance for a bowling arm that is loaded often. Evidence: shoulder evidence is limited and mixed (see the [shoulder section](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/injury-prevention/#site-shoulder) of the plyometrics injury page). Keep them light, controlled and pain-free.

Read each exercise page for its contraindications, regression and pain rule before using it. Some need a gym.

## What is not known

- No study of strength or trunk-endurance work with injury outcomes in adult club bowlers was found.
- No trial of any single exercise in this list in bowlers was found.
- The adult club trial's abstract does not give the exercises, the loads or how bowlers were allocated. It was 18 men over 4 weeks.
- Nothing was found on spin bowlers or masters bowlers, and the searches were not exhaustive. Women bowlers appear only in a small shoulder study on the [injury prevention page](/strength/cricket/injury-prevention/).
- Read but not published: a 2025 simulation study of 5% more strength (a model, not training), a 2024 correlation of core strength with balance, and a workload paper with implausibly wide intervals. Read but not used because a correction notice exists that was not read: a pre-season hamstring strength-testing meta-analysis.
- Every source was read by abstract only. No full text was read.

## Red flags

Stop and see a clinician for back pain that persists or keeps returning, especially in a bowler. Back pain with leg weakness, numbness around the saddle area, or new bladder or bowel changes is urgent. A sudden pop, pull or tearing in the side of the trunk or back of the thigh needs a clinician before you bowl or train again. Shoulder pain with bowling or throwing that disturbs sleep or builds across sessions also needs a clinician. The full list is on the [plyometrics injury-prevention page](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/injury-prevention/#red-flags). This page does not diagnose, rehabilitate or decide return to play.
