## What is researched now

This track is for **adults (18 and over) who play recreational or club cricket** and have been cleared to exercise. It is a draft, written from abstracts only, and it has not been reviewed by a physiotherapist, a strength and conditioning coach or a cricket-experienced reviewer. It does not diagnose, rehabilitate or decide return to play, and it makes no promise about speed, scoring or injury.

Researched drafts, each with who it is for, the evidence with grade and population, a short selection of existing exercises labelled as a convention, how it fits with the base plans, what is not known, and red flags:

- [Fast bowlers](/strength/cricket/fast-bowlers/): strength and trunk work, the back, workload and the hamstrings. One small adult club trial exists. Most evidence is professional or young.
- [Batters](/strength/cricket/batters/): upper-body strength and hitting distance, and how running between the wickets tires the legs. Small elite studies, no training study.
- [Injury prevention](/strength/cricket/injury-prevention/): the hamstring, shoulder and back evidence on the strength side. No cricket trial shows that an exercise prevents injury.
- [Season planning](/strength/cricket/season-planning/): what happened to strength across a season in one professional group, and a labelled convention for placing gym work.

Still stubs, with no exercise selection and a note of what exists: [spin bowlers](/strength/cricket/spin-bowlers/), [wicketkeepers](/strength/cricket/wicketkeepers/) and [fielders](/strength/cricket/fielders/).

## How to read the evidence here

- Cricket injury and training research is mostly observational. It shows associations, not proven causes.
- Almost all of it is in professional, academy or young players. Rates and training effects for adult club cricketers were mostly not found, and each claim states who was studied.
- Workload ratios are contested. Do not treat any single ratio, over count or delivery count as a rule or a limit.
- The strength blocks on the role pages are selections from existing exercises, set out as coaching conventions. No cricket study shows that any of them improves performance or lowers injury.
- Youth bowlers need qualified supervision. See the youth consensus claim below before programming for under-18s. The site does not programme for them.
- The base [programmes](/strength/programs/) and [weekly plan](/strength/weekly-plan/) are written for experienced lifters. The cricket pages are a selection from the same exercises and are not added on top of those plans. Beginners should start with [getting started](/getting-started/).

## Not published, and not searched

- Read but not published, because the abstract could not confirm what a claim needs: a 2025 strength simulation in bowlers, a 2024 core-strength and balance correlation, a workload paper with implausibly wide intervals, an uncontrolled throwing-training study with a cut-off abstract, and a hamstring strength-testing meta-analysis that has a correction notice that was not read.
- Not searched or not read: spin bowling, wicketkeeping and fielding in their own right, women's and masters cricket, concussion and protective equipment, heat and hydration, and full texts of every source.
