## Who this is for

Healthy adults (18 and over) who play recreational or club cricket and have been cleared to exercise. It is education and a labelled convention, not a periodised programme shown to work. It is not for under-18s, who need qualified supervision (see the [youth consensus claim](/strength/cricket/#claim-youth-training-consensus)), nor for anyone with a current or recent injury, a recent operation, pregnancy or the first months after birth, or a heart, lung, joint or nerve condition. Elite and professional players should work with a qualified coach.

## What the evidence says

All of the season evidence is from professional or elite men's cricket. No study of club cricketers across a season was found. The population is stated on each claim in the evidence list at the foot of the page.

- **Strength fell across a season.** In 12 male English county cricketers, back-squat strength and jump height rose over pre-season, and then strength, jump height and sprint performance fell across the competitive season ([claim](#claim-cricket-season-strength-decline), grade C). The authors concluded that the season's demands and the in-season training then in use did not give enough stimulus to maintain these qualities, and recommended more frequent, higher-load strength sessions in season. Nobody tested that recommendation, the group had no comparison, and a full professional schedule is not a club player's week.
- **The off-season allowed improvement.** In 14 elite male cricketers tested over a 20-week off-season, performance improved on nearly every fitness test ([claim](#claim-cricket-offseason-fitness), grade C). The training done is not described, so the gains cannot be tied to strength work.
- **Strength work in the off-season, without skills practice.** In 10 elite-academy pace bowlers, 8 weeks of off-season strength training raised strength measures and did not change release speed ([claim](#claim-cricket-offseason-strength-pace-bowlers), grade C, no control group).
- **Frequency may be flexible.** A meta-analysis in well-trained people found no clear difference in maximal strength gain between training frequencies over 6 to 12 weeks, and its authors suggest spreading the same weekly work over shorter, more frequent sessions to fit around competition ([claim](#claim-cricket-training-frequency-meta), grade B). It is not cricket, it measures maximal strength and not growth, and the suggestion is a hypothesis. For muscle growth the frequency evidence is a separate, mixed body of work on the [frequency principle](/strength/principles/frequency/).
- **Workload.** In professional fast bowlers, sudden increases in bowling and very low bowling were both associated with injury ([spikes](#claim-cricket-workload-spikes); [low and high](#claim-cricket-workload-low-and-high)), and tendon injuries followed sudden increases ([claim](#claim-cricket-workload-tissue-types)). The authors of that cohort recommend gradual upgrades, particularly early in a career. Workload ratios are contested ([claim](#claim-acwr-contested)). A first-class survey also linked a high number of overs in the week before a match to bowling injury ([claim](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-bowling-overs)). Those figures describe professionals and are not limits for you.
- **Match days, tournaments and travel.** No source was found.

## A season outline (a convention)

Nothing here is a study result. It is a cautious way to place the existing exercises, flagged for review by a strength and conditioning coach and a cricket-experienced reviewer. Role blocks are on the [fast bowlers](/strength/cricket/fast-bowlers/) and [batters](/strength/cricket/batters/) pages. Doses come from each exercise's page and the [volume](/strength/principles/volume/), [progression](/strength/principles/progression/) and [deloads](/strength/principles/deloads/) principles.

- **Off-season or pre-season (build).** Up to two short gym sessions a week from the role block. After any break, start on the easier regression of each exercise and build gradually. This is also the time to introduce anything that causes heavy soreness, such as the [Nordic hamstring curl](/strength/exercises/nordic-curl/), and not in the middle of the season.
- **In season (maintain).** One short session a week, or two shorter ones if the week allows, using the same few exercises with fewer sets and the loads kept close to normal, as in the deloads principle. Place it early in the match week. Do not do it the day before a match, and do not do it on a day you are still sore from the last one.
- **Congested weeks, tournaments and travel.** Use the lightest option, or skip the session, rather than add a make-up session later. No source gives a rule.
- **After time away (illness, holiday, injury).** Return to the easier regressions and build up gradually. Do not make up for lost sessions. After an injury, return is decided with a clinician.
- **End of season.** A few easier weeks before the next build, in the way the deloads principle describes. No cricket source supports a particular length.

A maintenance selection from existing exercises, a convention only: [goblet squat](/strength/exercises/goblet-squat/), [barbell hip thrust](/strength/exercises/hip-thrust/), [seated leg curl](/strength/exercises/seated-leg-curl/), [chest-supported row](/strength/exercises/chest-supported-row/) and [side plank](/strength/exercises/side-plank/). Read each page for its contraindications, regression and pain rule.

## How it fits with the other cricket pages and the base plans

- **Plyometrics cricket section.** The [two-session cricket power week](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/) uses its own match-week convention: short, early in the week, never the day before a match and never on consecutive days. It replaces the base plyometrics plan and is not added to it. Do not add its two sessions on top of two gym sessions without seeing how recovery feels, and in a week with both, start with one gym session. That is a convention, with no source.

## What is not known

- No study of in-season or pre-season strength work in club or recreational cricketers.
- No study of how to place gym work around match days, tournaments or travel, and no source for the length of the off-season rest.
- The decline in one small professional group is not shown to be due to too little strength work, and the recommended fix has not been tested.
- The season outline is a convention. The Nordic hamstring curl is introduced in the off-season by convention, with no cricket source. Read and not used: a seasonal-variation study in 15 young professionals and a survey of practices among South African coaches (title only).
- Every source was read by abstract only. No full text was read.

## Red flags

Stop and see a clinician for pain that builds across weeks of play, a sudden pop or tearing, loss of strength, numbness, or back pain with leg weakness or numbness around the saddle area (urgent). Do not train through persisting pain to hold on to a routine. The full tiers are on the [plyometrics injury-prevention page](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/injury-prevention/#red-flags). This page does not diagnose, rehabilitate or decide return to play.
