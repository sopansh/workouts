## Status

Stub, with no exercise selection. A dedicated fielding search has not been done.

What exists that is relevant:

- In elite men's domestic cricket in England and Wales, fielding was the second most common setting for match injuries, after bowling ([claim](#claim-cricket-domestic-injuries), grade C, professionals).
- Shoulder evidence is limited and mixed: a one-season elite cohort ([claim](#claim-cricket-shoulder-screening)) and a small study of elite women fast bowlers ([claim](#claim-cricket-shoulder-strength-female)). Neither tested a shoulder exercise.
- In 17 sub-elite men, throwing speed went with rotational medicine-ball throw speed, chest-pass distance, a lateral jump and shoulder internal-rotation strength, and not with squat or bench-press strength ([claim](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-throw-correlates), grade C, correlations only).
- Colliding with the boundary fence was named as a fielding risk ([claim](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/evidence/#claim-plyo-cricket-boundary-fence)). It concerns the ground, not training.
- The [plyometrics cricket pages](/plyometrics/sports/cricket/) cover landing, stopping and hip-led throwing work. The strength-side [injury prevention](/strength/cricket/injury-prevention/) and [season planning](/strength/cricket/season-planning/) pages cover what is known about shoulder, hamstring and back injury and the year.

Shoulder pain with throwing that disturbs sleep or builds across sessions needs a clinician. This page does not diagnose, rehabilitate or decide return to play.
