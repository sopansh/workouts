## What the evidence shows

Several short trials, mostly using MRI or ultrasound to measure muscle size, found that training muscles in a lengthened position produced more growth than training them in a shortened position:

- Overhead triceps extensions grew the triceps more than extensions with the arm at the side.
- Seated leg curls grew the hamstrings more than prone leg curls.
- Standing calf raises grew the gastrocnemius far more than seated calf raises.
- Deep squats grew the quadriceps more than shallow squats.
- Partial ranges at the stretched position grew parts of the quadriceps and the gastrocnemius at least as well as the full range.

## Limits

Most of these were small trials, many in untrained participants, and several used within-participant designs. They show a consistent direction but not a precise size of effect. The partial-range results do not mean shortening the range on compound lifts: those trials were single-joint exercises that started in the stretched position.
