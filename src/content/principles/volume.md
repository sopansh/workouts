## What a "hard set" means here

A hard set is a working set taken within roughly 0 to 3 reps of failure on a lift you can perform with good technique. Warm-up sets do not count. The set definition matters because the volume studies assume sets are taken close to failure.

## Direct and indirect sets

The [weekly plan](/strength/weekly-plan/) reports three numbers for every muscle:

- **Direct sets**: sets where the muscle is a primary mover, for example chest in a bench press.
- **Indirect sets**: sets where the muscle is a secondary helper, for example the triceps in a bench press.
- **Effective sets**: direct sets plus half the indirect sets. This follows the counting method that best fitted the largest meta-regression to date. It is a model, not a physiological law.

## Why a range and not a number

The dose-response data show a continuous slope with diminishing returns rather than a cliff. The best weekly volume for you depends on how quickly you recover, how much time you have and how many other muscles share the session.

## Limits

Most volume studies are short, in young adults, and many include untrained or lightly trained people. Whether an experienced lifter needs more or less is not settled.
