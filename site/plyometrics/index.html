Plyometrics

Basic, repeatable jump training

Two short sessions a week built from a few simple jumps, calf work and balance, repeated with small, patient increases. It uses only a clear floor, a couple of cones and an adjustable box. Everything starts on the floor.

2 sessions a week30–40 min8 weeks, repeatedDraft

Who this is for

Written for one experienced lifter who wants basic, repeatable work to build jump power and look after the feet, ankles and calves. It is for later, when a doctor or physiotherapist has said that jumping is appropriate. The strength section's beginner-only rule for squats, glutes and the lower back does not apply to jumping: here, everything starts at the beginning.

Equipment and the box

  • Floor. A stable, non-slip surface with clear space overhead. Do not jump on the futon. If the tatami is slippery or gives under your feet, choose another surface.
  • Cones. Used as flat markers for the side-to-side hop, about 30 to 40 cm apart. You do not jump over them.
  • Box. Yours can be set at roughly 50, 60 or 70 cm depending on the face. Those are all high for learning, so the first eight weeks do not use it. See when and whether to bring it in.

Fitting it round strength training

Allow at least 48 hours between the two sessions. Do the warm-up first, then the jumps, then any lifting, so the jumps are done fresh. The leg days in the paired split are the heaviest, so check how recovery feels before combining a jump session with one. This pairing is a suggestion, not tested advice.

The doses in this section are conservative planning conventions, not a validated prescription. Where evidence is cited it is graded on the fascia and tendons page.
