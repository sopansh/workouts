Progression

Eight weeks, then repeat

The plan builds by repetition. Only the jumps change over the eight weeks, and only slightly. Calf raises and balance stay at the same dose throughout.

The eight weeks

Foot contacts are the number of jump landings in a session (sets × reps summed across the jumps shown for that session). Warm-up squat-to-calf-raises, calf raises and balance work are not counted. These are planning conventions, not a validated prescription.

When to move up, hold or step back

  • Move up to the next row only if landings stayed quiet and controlled throughout, and you felt comfortable during and the day after both sessions.
  • Hold the current row for another week if landings got noisy, you were unusually sore or tired the next day, or you missed a session.
  • Step back a row, or return to the regression, after illness, a break of more than a couple of weeks, or any pain. If pain does not settle within 2 to 3 weeks, see a clinician.
  • After week 8 do not add more. Repeat weeks 5 to 8, or take an easier week, and keep the landings clean. The aim here is repetition, not escalation.

The box

Your box can be set at roughly 50, 60 or 70 cm. Those heights are high for learning, so this plan does not use it, and nothing in the eight weeks depends on it. It is optional.

  • Consider it only after the floor jumps feel comfortable and controlled for several weeks, and ideally after a coach or physiotherapist has seen you jump.
  • Check the box is stable in the chosen orientation and that you have clear, non-slip space to land on.
  • Use it only as a place to step onto and step down from. Do not jump down off it, and do not do depth jumps.
  • Treat any box work as a separate decision with its own review. This plan gives no box prescription.
