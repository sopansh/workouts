Fascia and tendons

Fascia, calves and tendons

Fascia is connective tissue, not a separate muscle, so it cannot be trained in isolation. This page says what the plan does for the calves, ankles and feet, and what nobody can honestly promise.

What fascia is

Each skeletal muscle sits inside layers of connective tissue that blend into its tendon, and fascia is the connective tissue between the skin and bones. The tension a muscle produces passes through these layers to the tendon. See the background claim.

What the plan does

  • Starts easy. Bounces and jumps are small, rhythmic and repeated, so the tissue is loaded gradually.
  • Builds calf basics. Straight-knee and bent-knee calf raises work the two main calf muscles, and are slow and controlled.
  • Adds balance. Single-leg standing loads the ankle and foot stabilisers with no impact.

These choices are reasonable and cautious. They are not shown to produce a particular fascia adaptation.

What nobody can promise

  • No source gathered for this plan shows that these exercises change fascia, reduce injury risk or add a particular number of centimetres to a jump.
  • The tendon review measured tendon size and stiffness in healthy adults, not injuries.
  • Stretching ranked low in that review for the outcomes studied. The plan therefore does not rely on stretching for tendon health.

Evidence and grades

Grades follow the strength section's rubric. Both journal sources had their DOIs checked on Crossref (title, year and retraction notices) and their abstracts read. Checking a DOI does not show the claim is perfectly applied here, so the limits are stated with each claim.
